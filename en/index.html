Step by step

How a foreigner buys property in Brazil

1Get a CPFThe CPF is the Brazilian taxpayer number. You can request it at a Brazilian consulate, and it is all you need to sign the contract.
2Choose remotelyRicardo sends a short selection with videos, floor plans and payment terms. In some projects, the developer flies clients in on a private plane to visit.
3Send the funds legallyThe money enters Brazil through an official foreign exchange contract with an authorized bank, and the investment is registered with the Central Bank of Brazil.
4Contract and deedYou can sign in person or through a legal representative with a power of attorney. The deed is registered at the local property registry.
5Costs and taxesBesides the price, there is the ITBI transfer tax and notary and registry fees. Ricardo gives you the estimate for each property.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. For your specific case, talk to a Brazilian lawyer or accountant.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners buy property in Brazil?

Yes. Foreigners can buy urban property in Brazil, such as apartments and houses on the coast, with the same ownership rights as Brazilians. Restrictions apply mainly to rural land and border areas, which is not the case of Itapema and Porto Belo.

Do I need to live in Brazil or have a visa?

No. You do not need residency or a visa to buy. You need a CPF, the Brazilian taxpayer number, which can be requested at a Brazilian consulate.

How do I send the money to Brazil?

The funds must enter Brazil through an official foreign exchange contract with an authorized bank. The investment is registered with the Central Bank of Brazil, which documents its origin and makes it easier to send the money back when you sell. A bank or a local accountant can guide you.

Can I get a mortgage in Brazil?

Brazilian banks rarely finance non-residents. That is why most foreign buyers use the developer's payment plan: a down payment, installments during construction and a payment at handover, all in Brazilian reais.

Can I buy without traveling to Brazil?

Yes. The choice can be made by video and WhatsApp, and the deed can be signed by a legal representative with a power of attorney. Many buyers still prefer to visit before signing, and in some projects the developer flies clients in on a private plane.

Can I rent the property out?

Yes. Rental income is taxed in Brazil, and non-residents usually appoint a local representative for tax matters. Ask a Brazilian accountant about the best structure for your case.
